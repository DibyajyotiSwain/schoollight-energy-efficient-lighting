# Design-thinking process

The original presentation uses six stages to structure the project.

## 1. Empathize

Understand the needs of students and staff through surveys, interviews, workshops, and a Google Form feedback tool. The intent is to collect perspectives on lighting preferences and challenges in different school spaces.

## 2. Define

Translate feedback into clear goals: improve energy efficiency, support a conducive learning environment, and adapt lighting to different activities and areas. Priority spaces identified in the presentation include classrooms, corridors, and common areas.

## 3. Ideate

Explore a personalized smart-lighting control system with automated consumption sensors. The concept proposes user-adjustable brightness, colour temperature, and lighting patterns, supported by automation.

## 4. Prototype

The presentation includes a planning blueprint and lighting concept visuals. These communicate the proposed direction; they are not construction-ready engineering drawings or tested prototypes.

## 5. Implement (proposed)

The presentation outlines an initial pilot in the IBCP Block, with different lighting approaches for classrooms, corridors, laboratories, and the auditorium. It then proposes a phased expansion to other blocks, subject to evaluation.

## 6. Test (proposed)

The proposed evaluation considers energy consumption, lighting quality, and user feedback. Findings would inform iterative adjustments and refinements.

## Process note

The source presentation calls the approach a six-stage process and includes both a pilot/implementation stage and a testing/refinement stage. This documentation retains that structure while making clear that the pilot and testing are proposed, not verified as completed.
